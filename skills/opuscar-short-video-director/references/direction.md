# Cinematic direction with restraint

Choose a small, coherent grammar for the film. Cinematic does not mean constant movement, fake shallow focus, excessive shake or a technique at every cut. Use the primary Opuscar style's native moves and preserve its visual invariants.

## Shot sequences

- Establishing wide → medium action → close detail.
- Action → reaction → consequence.
- Detail → wider reveal.
- Character viewpoint → character response.
- Preparation → event → result.

Select what the supplied story needs. A scene might need only two of these. A reaction or insert must convey new information. Vary shot scale/angle/composition between adjacent views; repeatedly zooming the same illustration is not meaningful variety.

## Camera specification

For each shot define **start frame, end frame, target, path, direction, speed/easing and motivation**. Preserve the subject's headroom, lead room, floor contact and intended reading area. Specify whether motion is a real perspective move, crop/zoom, parallax or camera pan; do not claim an orbit from one flat illustration.

| Move | Narrative use | Execution check |
|---|---|---|
| Push-in | emphasis, discovery, tension | controlled acceleration; stable focus |
| Pull-back | reveal context, consequence or scale | new information actually appears |
| Lateral tracking | accompany a person/car | subject/ground share coordinates |
| Pan or tilt | follow a look or disclose detail | start/end motivated; no aimless drift |
| Layered parallax | depth in 2D/2.5D | near layers move more; no exposed edges |
| Limited arc | change spatial understanding | requires real geometry/extra viewpoints |
| Locked camera | readable teaching or strong internal action | intentional staging, not frozen filler |

Wide frames establish geography; mediums show action; close-ups show emotion; inserts show objects/words. Low angles can suggest power, high angles vulnerability or route geography. POV/over-shoulder views connect observation and reaction. Dutch angles/shake are exceptional, not a default for energy.

Runner/car motion must follow the road in depth, including size/perspective change and plausible orientation. Do not slide characters across the road's width. Keep all following effects through the same world-to-screen transform.

## Cuts

- **Cut on action:** finish the same gesture in the incoming view.
- **Graphic/match cut:** connect meaningful shapes/compositions, not a random resemblance.
- **Match on motion:** preserve movement direction and speed across views.
- **J-cut:** incoming scene sound/narration begins over outgoing picture.
- **L-cut:** outgoing sound continues over incoming picture.
- **Reaction/insert/cutaway:** show relevant response, detail or context.
- **Smash cut:** use a motivated contrast for surprise/comedy.
- **Cross-cut:** alternate related actions while geography/time remain understandable.

J/L-cuts do not mean delaying subtitles behind speech. Keep subtitles anchored to voice and offset the visual cut or contextual sound. Record the lead/tail and its purpose. A clean hard cut often works best.

## Seamless options

Plan outgoing and incoming images together before generating/drawing assets:

- foreground object/occlusion hides the cut;
- matching whip-pan direction/speed;
- push into/out of a compatible shape or surface;
- foreground wipe motivated by a passing subject;
- shape or texture match;
- a continuous sound bridge across different pictures.

Specify matching position, scale, direction, exposure, color and transition duration. Do not use a morph that changes character anatomy or breaks the learning example. Fallback to a clean cut when a convincing transition would cost clarity or require unavailable assets.

## Continuity and B-roll

Maintain the 180-degree axis, screen direction, eyelines, light, costumes/props and scene geography. Use a sufficiently different angle/scale to avoid accidental jump cuts; the 30-degree rule is a guide, not a decorative requirement. Intentional exceptions need a reason.

B-roll may establish location, explain a sentence, show a detail/consequence or relieve repetitive narration shots. Use illustrated inserts or compatible licensed footage where the style/brief permits. Match color, texture, framing and rhythm. Do not add footage just to fill seconds; preserve the Opuscar-first approach.

For every prominent technique/B-roll choice, write a one-line reason in the storyboard. The subtraction pass removes choices without a clear benefit. Keep one primary visual event per beat and a small family of transitions, with quiet moments around learning.
